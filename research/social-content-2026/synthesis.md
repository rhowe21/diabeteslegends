# SYNTHESIS — 9 theses

## Corpus inventory
- Library A "Rob's Content Inspo & Content Strategy" — 279 items, 22 Jun–26 Aug 2026, 195 distinct creators
- Library B "Rob's AI Content Repository" — 110 items, same window, Claude-centric (231 Claude / 1 ChatGPT)
- Combined 389 items. Filing rate ~93/mo, steady. Top 20 creators = 28% of Library A.
- Platform mix (A): 68.3% IG Reels, 24.8% carousels, 4.7% static, 1.1% YouTube, 0.4% Threads.
- First-party: 80 @robhowe21 IG posts, 9 @diabeticsdoingthings posts, 320 YouTube videos.

## T1 — The library is a mirror, not a map
53.6% of Library A is content-strategy creators teaching content strategy. Health/science
3.6%, fitness 3.6%, sports 1.4%. Approximately zero items come from the categories
Recreation Dallas actually serves (transportation, CPG/food, financial services).
The corpus is selected from people whose business model is selling growth courses to
creators. Their tactics are therefore optimised for the metric that sells courses —
comment volume converted to a DM list — not for the outcomes a brand client buys
(purchase intent, footfall, retention, cost per asset). Using this library as a client
playbook imports someone else's objective function.

## T2 — The comment-keyword gap is the largest collect/practice divergence
26.3% of Library A (73 items) is built on comment-keyword-to-DM gating. It is the single
most concentrated mechanic in the corpus, and the evidence in the corpus is striking:
Leif Abel 6,974 comments vs 1,540 likes; Ibra AI 5,443 vs 1,198; Valeria Ramirez 1,450 vs
1,854; lauren dudley 865 vs 1,153. The mechanic inverts the like:comment ratio.
Rob used it 0 times in 80 posts.
Caveat that matters: it is not automatically right for him. It monetises an info-product
audience. The transferable part is not the keyword — it is that these creators always
have ONE owned destination (DM list, newsletter, waitlist). Rob has none.

## T3 — Format allocation is inverted against returns
87.5% of output is Reels — his weakest format on every measured axis.
Carousels: 3.0x reel median reach, 16x saves, 14x shares, and 13 measured follows/post.
Static images (n=2): 7.8x reel median reach.
Instagram does not report media_follows for Reels at all, so the only formats where
follow-attribution is *provable* are the two he barely uses. Exactly one item in the
279-item library argues this (Katelyn Rhoades: "if I started over I would not post
reels"). He filed the minority view and did not act on it.

## T4 — The hook thesis is true, and it is measurable
Splitting his 70 reels at the median skip rate:
  better hooks (avg skip 0.406) -> median reach 2,580
  worse  hooks (avg skip 0.642) -> median reach   910
2.8x reach difference attributable to the first three seconds. Account median skip rate
0.534 — more than half of all viewers leave before second three.
This is the commercially important finding: it converts "good hook" from an aesthetic
opinion into a number. Skip rate is a leading indicator a client can be held to, and it
is available before reach resolves.

## T5 — Effort does not equal outcome; structure does
Rob's own July 7 note to the team: ":3-:10 of B-roll with an on screen Hook/Copy can
drive more views/follows/engagements than a post that took 10 people 50 hours each."
His data agrees: best static image (zero video production) = 16,429 reach vs median
reel 1,246. The corpus agrees from a dozen directions (Oscar Louis, Tom/bonusfootage,
Michael Smoak, Kien Nguyen's B-roll bank, itsmodernmillie's one-clip-many-angles).
This is already house doctrine at Recreation Dallas. It is not yet priced or productised.

## T6 — Two platforms, two different reward functions, one fuel
YouTube: 320 videos, 57,231 views, 66 subscribers, MEDIAN 5 views/video. 67% of videos
under 20 views. 88% gained zero subscribers.
But the subscriber drivers are unambiguous and they are not the personality content:
  13 subs — "Lesser Known features of the Medtronic MiniMed 780G System"
   3 subs — "Instinct Sensor Insertion"
   2 subs — "Medtronic Guardian Sensor 3 Insertion — How to insert"
   2 subs — "What is SmartGuard Auto Mode and how does it work"
Evergreen searchable device utility converts. The IG breakout ("21 years with T1D")
did 79,100 reach on Instagram and 4,283 views on YouTube — 18x worse.
Cross-posting is being run as distribution when it is actually category error.

## T7 — The power law demands re-exploitation, not just volume
Top 1 post = 25.0% of all reach in the window. Top 10 (12.5% of output) = 60.1%.
Two consequences, and the second is the one being missed:
(a) volume matters because outliers cannot be predicted, only bought;
(b) the return on systematically sequelling a winner dwarfs the return on a new
    at-bat — and the Jul 2 post (79,100 reach) has no sequel anywhere in the data.
Velocity confirms the cost: 60.7 new followers/day (wk Jul 22–28) decaying to
11.6/day (wk Aug 13–19), -81% in four weeks. The July surge was one post, not a rate change.

## T8 — The AI operations corpus is the actual moat
Library B is a 110-item map of running a content operation on Claude: skills, Cowork,
Code, MCP, Windsor.ai connectors, Manychat, automated editing, brand-identity systems,
analytics dashboards, content-mentor audits.
Recreation Dallas has already BUILT the things most of these creators are only
describing: a Slack->Notion content filer watching three channels, a weekly automated
growth agent producing velocity reports and filing ideas, live Windsor.ai connectors on
both IG accounts and YouTube.
Most agencies are talking about AI. This one is running it. That gap is sellable and
it has a shelf life.

## T9 — The library is a collection, not a system
14 duplicate entries (5%), 3 items unreadable (auth walls), several with captions never
captured. No dedupe, no re-check, no status field, no link from "saved" to "tested" to
"measured". 389 items in, zero closed loops out.
The product should be the loop the library is missing.
