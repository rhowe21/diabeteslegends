The Skip Rate Standard

The library is not wrong. It is aimed at someone else. Fifty-four percent of it is content-strategy creators teaching content strategy — people whose business is selling growth courses, whose tactics are tuned to the metric that sells those courses.

The value in it is not the tactics. It is that when you test the library's central claim against our own numbers, the claim holds and becomes measurable. That number is the product.

Findings — ordered by consequence

F1

Hook quality is real, and it is a number

Split the 70 reels on @robhowe21 at their median skip rate — the share of viewers who leave inside three seconds — and the two halves are not close.

A 2.8× difference in reach, decided in the first three seconds. The account's median skip rate is 0.534 — more than half of everyone who sees a post leaves before it starts. Median watch time is 8.9s.

Why this one matters mostEvery other finding here is a recommendation. This one is an instrument. Skip rate resolves within hours, before reach does, and it converts “good hook” from a matter of taste into a figure a client can be measured against. Nothing else in the library does that.

Carousels return 3.0× the reach of reels, 16× the saves and 14× the shares. And there is a measurement trap underneath: Instagram does not report follow attribution for reels at all. The only two formats where follows can be proven are the two barely being used.

Exactly one item in the 279-item library argues this — Katelyn Rhoades, “if I started over I would not post reels.” The minority view was filed and not acted on.

Read this carefullyTwo static images is not a sample. The honest claim is that the format taking 2.5% of the effort has never been given a fair test — not that it is 7.8× better. The action is to run the test, not to rebuild the calendar around n=2.

The 2 July post — “21 years with T1D and here's what I wish people understood” — did 79,100 reach, 605 saves and 938 shares. It is a quarter of the entire quarter.

The usual lesson drawn from a power law is post more, because outliers can't be predicted, only bought. That is half right and it is the half already being done. The missed half: the single highest-return act available is sequelling a winner, and that post has no sequel anywhere in the data.

Follower velocity shows what the omission costs. It ran at 60.7/day the week of 22 July and 11.6/day the week of 13 August — down 81% in four weeks. The July surge was one post, not a change in rate.

F4

The library's dominant mechanic has never been used once

Comment-keyword-to-DM gating — “comment HOOKS and I'll send it” — is the most concentrated pattern in the collection: 73 of 279 items, 26.3%. The evidence inside the library is unusually clean, because the mechanic inverts a ratio that normally never inverts.

The largest collect-versus-practise divergence in the setA quarter of everything filed over ten weeks is built on one mechanic. It has been used zero times in eighty posts.

But copying it would be the wrong move, and this is where the library's bias bites. The keyword works because it converts an info-product audience into a DM list that can be sold a course. That is not this business.

The transferable part is not the keyword. It is that every one of these creators has exactly one owned destination — a list, a waitlist, a newsletter — and every post points at it. Rob has none. The right adaptation is a keyword pointed at something real: clinic registration, the podcast's episode archive, a device-question inbox.

F5

Two platforms, two reward functions, one fuel

YouTube is being fed cross-posted Instagram personality content, where it dies. 320 videos in the window produced 57,231 views and 66 subscribers. Median views per video: 5. Two thirds of the catalogue is under 20 views; 88% gained no subscriber at all.

But the videos that do convert are unambiguous, and they are not the personality content.

Instagram pays for first-person emotional narrative. YouTube pays for evergreen, searchable device utility, because YouTube is a search engine wearing a feed. Same fuel into two different machines is not distribution; it is a category error with a publishing schedule.

F6

House doctrine is correct and unpriced

:3–:10 of B-roll with an on-screen Hook/Copy can drive more views, follows and engagements than a post that took 10 people 50 hours each. Effort does not equal content success in organic content at this current juncture.Rob Howe · #greyhound · 7 July 2026

The data agrees without qualification. The best static image of the quarter — zero video production — did 16,429 reach against a median reel of 1,246. A dozen items in the library converge on the same point from different angles: the B-roll bank, one clip reshot from nine angles, “stop filming every day.”

This is already what Recreation Dallas believes and tells clients. It has never been turned into evidence, a number, or a line item — which is the whole opportunity in section two.

That is a selection effect with teeth. These creators are selected for being good at teaching creators, and their tactics optimise for comment volume and DM capture because that is what sells a course. Handing this library to a brand client as a playbook imports someone else's objective function into their marketing.

The library is still worth keeping. It is a good map of craft — hooks, structure, pacing, formats — and a poor map of objectives. Those need separating before any of it reaches a client deck.

F8

The AI operations corpus is the part competitors can't copy yet

The second library — 110 items over the same weeks — is a map of running a content operation on Claude: skills, Cowork, Code, MCP connectors, automated editing, brand-identity systems, analytics dashboards. It mentions Claude 231 times and ChatGPT once.

The asymmetryMost of those 110 posts are creators describing a system. Recreation Dallas has already built one: a Slack-to-Notion filer watching three channels, a weekly automated growth agent that writes velocity reports and files content ideas, and live Windsor.ai connectors on both Instagram accounts and YouTube. This entire report was produced by that stack in an evening.

Most agencies are talking about AI. This one is running it, and the gap has a shelf life measured in quarters, not years.

F9

389 items in, zero loops closed

The filer works. What's missing is everything after it. There are 14 duplicate entries — one post filed three separate times — three items that are permanently unreadable behind auth walls, and several whose captions were never captured at all. There is no status field, no dedupe, no re-check.

More importantly there is no path from saved to tried to measured. Ten weeks of daily collection has produced no recorded test. The one time the loop nearly closed is instructive: the growth agent filed nine ideas across two weeks and flagged, in its own words, that none of them are getting filmed.

The product in section two is that missing loop.
